# How to Photograph Items So They Trade Faster

People can't touch your item through a screen. Your photos do all the convincing. Listings with clear, honest photos get dramatically more offers than listings with one dark blurry shot. You don't need a fancy camera — your phone is enough. You just need to do it right.

## Use natural daylight

Take your photos near a big window or outside in open shade. Daylight shows true colors and details that indoor lamps wash out or tint yellow. Avoid direct harsh sunlight, which creates hard shadows — a cloudy day or a shaded porch is perfect. Never use flash; it flattens everything and hides detail.

## Clean the item first

Wipe it down. This takes two minutes and it's the highest-return step in this whole guide. Dust, fingerprints, and smudges make even good items look neglected. A clean item photographs like it's worth more — because it looks like it was cared for.

## Clear the background

You don't need a studio. A plain wall, a clean table, a bedsheet, or a tidy floor works fine. Move the laundry, the dishes, and the random clutter out of the frame. The item should be the only thing the viewer looks at. If the background is messy, people wonder what else you're careless about.

## Shoot every angle

Minimum five photos for most items:

1. Front view, straight on
2. Back view
3. Both sides
4. Top and bottom (or a three-quarter angle showing depth)
5. Close-up of any important detail — brand label, model number, screen, stitching

For bigger items like bikes or furniture, add a wide shot showing the whole thing in context. More photos answer questions before they're asked, which means more offers and fewer wasted meetups.

## Show flaws honestly — close up

This feels backwards, but it's the most powerful trust-builder you have. That scratch on the corner? Photograph it clearly. The worn handle? Show it. When buyers discover flaws in person that weren't in the photos, trades fall apart and everyone wasted a trip. When flaws are shown upfront, the person who shows up already accepted them — and the trade goes through.

Honest photos also let you hold firm on your asking value. "Yes, there's a scratch, it's right there in photo 6" ends haggling fast.

## Prove it works

A photo of a powered-off laptop tells nothing. Turn the screen on and photograph it working. Show the bike's gears, the tool plugged in and running, the watch telling time. For anything with a screen, a photo of it lit up is worth ten photos of it dark. Short videos are even better if the platform supports them.

## Include something for scale

"I thought it was bigger" kills trades. Put a common object next to it — a coin, a pen, your hand — or just say the dimensions in the description. For clothes and shoes, photograph the size tag clearly.

## What NOT to do

- Don't use the manufacturer's stock photos. Everyone knows what it looked like new; they want to see YOUR item.
- Don't use heavy filters. If the color in the photo doesn't match real life, that's a failed trade waiting to happen.
- Don't photograph through a dirty lens. Wipe your phone camera first — it's the cause of half of all blurry listing photos.
- Don't post just one photo. One photo looks like you're hiding something, even when you're not.

## The order matters

Put your best, clearest, most attractive shot first — that's the thumbnail everyone sees while browsing. Then follow with the angles, the details, the proof-it-works shot, and finally the honest flaw close-ups. Think of it as a story: here's the item, here's everything about it, here's proof, and here's my honesty.

Listings with this kind of photo set stand out anywhere, including on Valora, where good photos are often the difference between a listing that sits and a listing that gets three offers in a day.

## The short version

Daylight, clean item, clean background, five-plus angles, honest flaw shots, proof it works. Thirty minutes of effort, and your item trades twice as fast.
