# How to Trade Items Safely in Person

Trading face-to-face is one of the best parts of swapping items — you see exactly what you're getting before you agree to anything. But meeting a stranger always carries some risk. These simple steps keep your trades safe and comfortable.

## Meet in a public place

Never invite a stranger to your home, and never go to theirs. Choose a busy public spot instead. Good options:

- A police station parking lot. Many police stations now have marked "safe trade zones" with cameras, made exactly for buy/sell/trade meetups.
- A busy shopping center or grocery store parking lot during the day.
- A coffee shop where you can sit down and look the item over.

Pick a place you already know, so you're comfortable there.

## Go during the day

Daylight makes everything easier. You can inspect the item properly, there are more people around, and public places are busier. If the other person can only meet late at night, suggest a well-lit 24-hour location like a gas station or a fast-food restaurant — or just pick another day.

## Bring someone with you

A friend changes the whole dynamic. Two people are harder to pressure than one, and a second pair of eyes helps you check the item. If nobody can come, at least tell someone exactly where you're going, who you're meeting, and when you expect to be back. Share your live location from your phone while you're there.

## Inspect before you agree

The whole point of meeting in person is that you don't have to take anyone's word for it. Take your time:

- Look the item over from every angle.
- Turn it on. Plug it in. Try every button, zipper, latch, or moving part.
- For a bike, ride it around the parking lot. For a phone or laptop, check the screen, battery, and ports.
- Ask about anything that looks worn or damaged.

A fair trader expects this. If someone rushes you or says "just take it, it's fine," that's a reason to slow down, not speed up.

## Keep your personal details private

You don't need to share your home address, your workplace, or your daily routine to complete a trade. Agree on the meeting spot through the platform's messages and keep it there. Don't send photos of your ID or personal documents — a trade doesn't require any of that.

## Handle cash-on-top carefully

Many trades include a little cash to balance the value — you trade your old bike plus $50 for a better one. Agree on the exact amount before you meet so there's no awkward negotiation in the parking lot. Bring exact change when you can. Count it together, out in the open, before you hand over the item.

## Trust your instincts

If something feels wrong — the person shows up with extra people you weren't told about, the item doesn't match the listing, or you're being pressured — you are allowed to walk away. No trade is worth an unsafe situation. A polite "I've changed my mind, thanks anyway" is enough. You don't owe anyone an explanation.

## Keep a record

After the trade, mark it complete on the platform. On Valora, for example, accepted trade offers are recorded with the agreed terms and the platform fee, so both sides have a clear record of what happened. A short message like "trade complete, item as described" protects everyone if there's ever a disagreement later.

## The short version

Public place, daytime, bring a friend, inspect carefully, keep details private, and walk away if it feels wrong. Do those six things and in-person trading is about as safe as a hobby gets.
